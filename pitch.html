The loop

Every second you spend down there is a second you need to get back.

Most games with a timer use it to hurry you. This one uses it to make you wrong. The air is the only resource, and it is spent on everything at once: the fight, the chest, and the walk home.

So the game is not really about combat. It is about a decision you make with a number in the corner of your eye — I have eleven seconds, and the shaft is four seconds behind me, so this chest is not mine. Getting that decision wrong is how you lose, and it is the most interesting thing a player does.

Go down

Five environments, fixed geometry, reshuffled encounters. Deeper stone is worth more.

Take what you can

Gems and gold out of chests and monsters. Everything is carried, nothing is banked.

Get out

Any exit banks the whole bag. Dying or suffocating loses all of it — and only it.

The design

The biggest tank you can buy is deliberately not enough.

Thirty seconds to start. Ten upgrades take it to three and a half minutes, and three and a half minutes still will not walk the whole dungeon and beat the boss. That is not a balance problem — it is the point.

A careful clear taking every chest, with all four shortcuts open, lands five seconds inside that ceiling. Closing the gap needs shortcuts, and a shortcut only opens from a lever placed past the ground it skips. So progress cannot be bought, cannot be rushed, and cannot be learned from a walkthrough video: you have to have been there.

Money never buys more than the last 30% of anything. Gold covers a gem shortfall only once you already hold 70% of what a purchase needs, measured per grade — so no amount of grinding the easy ground substitutes for going deep.

Safety

A score is never sent to us. The keystrokes are.

Leaderboards and an in-game economy are the two things players cheat, and the two things an investor should ask about first. Here is the whole mechanism.

01

The server opens the run

It issues the seed — which decides the entire layout — and writes down the tank and the equipment before a single frame is played. The browser chooses none of it.

02

You play. The game records the keys.

Not positions, not scores — which keys were held on which tick. The simulation is a deterministic function of exactly that.

03

The server replays it

Through the same code, from the conditions it wrote down itself, and works out what happened — the loot, the time, the death.

04

The result is credited from the replay

Both the leaderboard score and the loot. There is no request anywhere in the game that adds to a balance by asking.

To cheat, you would have to submit keystrokes that genuinely produce the score. That is not cheating. That is playing.

This is why the simulation was built as a pure reducer at a fixed sixty ticks a second before anything else was built at all. Determinism was not an engineering preference — it is the thing that makes an honest leaderboard and a sellable economy possible later.

Where it is

The art is generated, not licensed. Every sprite comes out of fourteen Python scripts checked in beside their output, so there is no asset bill and no licence to honour. The sound is the same idea: every noise is synthesised the moment it plays. There is not one audio file in the project.

Infrastructure costs effectively nothing. Stateless serverless and managed Postgres, with no always-on process — chosen at the architecture stage so it stays true as players arrive.

What it is not

The missing pieces, before you ask.

A founder who knows what is not built is easier to back than one who does not. This list is also in the public repository, where it has to stay true.

  • Not built

    Monetisation. No payment provider, no checkout. The economy it would sell into is finished and server-owned — which had to be right first, because selling currency on top of an economy the client can write is selling something you do not control.

  • Not built

    PvP and Survival. Both are on the menu and marked as coming, so a player can see the shape of the game. Nothing is behind either.

  • Not built

    Mobile. Keyboard only. Web first as validation, native app after — but that is a plan, not a build.

  • Early

    Players. It went live this week. The numbers on the boards are real runs, and there are not many of them yet.

Why partner

One person built a deterministic action game with an economy that cannot be edited from a browser console, and it is live.

The hard part is done, and it is the part that is usually skipped. Most games reach a playable build with a client-trusted economy and discover the problem when money is involved. This one solved it first, which is why the next three things on the list are additions rather than rewrites.

What money buys is time and reach: finishing 1v1, standing up payments properly, and putting it in front of players. Everything the product needs in order to charge for something is already true.
