- (decision) RESOLVED - timer's role. Timer is 'unseen horror': ambient pressure, not a watched number. Its job is to degrade decision quality - it rushes the player, denies them focus, and causes mistakes and damage. Causal chain is timer -> rushing -> mistakes -> HP loss -> death. Timer and HP are NOT competing tension systems; the timer kills THROUGH combat rather than by expiring. This sharpens the brief, which framed the timer as pressure/difficulty/progression gate but not as a decision-quality degrader.
- (decision) Multiple EXITS placed throughout the dungeon, not a single entrance/exit. Player extracts via a nearby exit rather than backtracking the full distance. Complements the already-locked shortcuts + skip items as the second half of the re-traversal answer: shortcuts compress the way IN, exits compress the way OUT. Matches DRG drop-pod pattern from research.
- (decision) Gear upgrades must be LEGIBLE in moment-to-moment survival. Aziza's 1-HP beat is explicitly caused by a pre-run gear purchase: without the upgrade Maya dies, with it she survives at 1 HP. Gear is a survivability margin the player can feel, not an abstract stat. Design implication - the payoff moment for a purchase should be perceptible in a fight, not only in reachable distance.
- (decision) FICTION ESTABLISHED - the timer is OXYGEN, not an abstract clock. The dungeon is saturated with a virus that transformed its inhabitants into the monsters. The player enters wearing an oxygen mask; the countdown is the air supply. This makes the timer diegetic rather than arbitrary, which the research named as the single biggest defence against a timer feeling unfair. It also retro-justifies the 'corrupt archers' enemy naming - corruption is viral infection.
- (decision) TIMER UI: large, centre-top, always visible. 'Unseen horror' meant ambient dread, NOT a hidden clock - correction to earlier reading. Final 10 seconds: screen corners darken progressively inward as oxygen depletes, a diegetic vignette closing in.
- (decision) FAIL STATE on oxygen depletion: the player TRANSFORMS INTO A MONSTER from breathing the virus. Not a generic death screen. Thematically fuses the fail state with the world's premise - you become what you were fighting.
- (decision) LOOT MODEL: two sources - chests (opened) and gems (mined). Gems are GRADED by distance: grade 1 near the entrance usable only on weak gear, grade rising the deeper you go, so higher grades unlock more powerful upgrades. Chest DENSITY also rises with distance. Small chance of a LEGENDARY CHEST containing loot that should not be reachable until far later - a jackpot/lottery beat. Together these are the economic argument for pushing deeper: better grade, more volume, and a jackpot chance.

If grade-1 gems only work on weak gear, they become dead weight once the player outgrows the entrance band - exactly the Archero failure mode the economy research flagged (players left holding currency with nothing to spend it on). Needs either a sink, an upcycle/combine path, or a conversion rate.

- (decision) RESOLVED Q2 - bailing early costs NOTHING. Exits are location-agnostic and equivalent; leaving through any exit banks everything carried. The only cost of leaving is opportunity cost - forgone depth, grade and legendary rolls. Cost therefore sits on the PUSH side, not the bail side: pushing risks the carried loot to death. Extraction-shooter model (Tarkov/DRG), matches research note that tension comes from the loot delta rather than the clock.
- (decision) SHORTCUT UNLOCK RULE - a shortcut is not usable on sight. Reaching the far side of the segment it skips reveals a LEVER; flicking it opens that shortcut's door permanently for future runs. Rationale from Crusher: players who learn shortcut locations from videos would otherwise skip content they have not earned, defeating the point - shortcuts exist to remove boredom from ground you have ALREADY solved, not to sell a head start.
- (event) OPEN Q11 raised - the lever rule conflicts with purchasable SKIP ITEMS locked upstream in the brief. If a skip item jumps a player past ground they never traversed, money reproduces exactly the video-knowledge exploit the lever was invented to close, and brushes the F2P 'no purchase-exclusive power' constraint. Skip items likely must be gated to already-levered shortcuts. Not yet raised with Crusher.

- (decision) RESOLVED Q11 - 'purchasable' skip items are bought with GEMS EARNED DURING THE RUN, not with real money. This closes the pay-to-skip hole: skipping is paid for in earned in-run currency, is consumable rather than permanent, and unlocks nothing. The lever rule (permanent shortcut access) stays strictly earned-by-traversal.
- (decision) POTIONS established as a system - purchased with gems, extremely valuable and expensive, an open catalogue. Named so far: full heal, grant time/oxygen, skip an area. Potions are the in-run spend and the reason to carry gems rather than bank them.
- (decision) DUNGEON SEGMENTATION - the dungeon is 5 environments/parts. Each environment contains 1-2 shortcuts. This is the first concrete structural spec for the distance axis and gives shortcut placement a frame (5-10 shortcuts total in v1).
- (decision) SKIP POTION mechanics - advances the player to the next environment regardless of remaining distance; if already near the boundary it carries further, to the next shortcut. Its cost is LOOT: everything in the skipped stretch is forfeited. Trades loot for time, which is the exact inverse of the push-or-bail trade (safety for depth). Both decisions run on the same axis.
- (event) OBSERVATION - potions costing gems may be the answer to Q1 (gem-grade obsolescence). If potions are priced in gems, outgrown low-grade gems get a permanent non-saturating sink, which is exactly the anti-Archero consumable sink the economy research recommended. Needs confirming with Crusher: do potions cost gems of any grade, or a separate currency?
- (decision) RESOLVED Q1 (gem-grade obsolescence) - potions cost a COMBINATION of gems, not a single grade. High-grade gems alone cannot buy a potion; low grades stay permanently required as ingredients. Low-grade gems therefore never become dead weight, which kills the Archero failure mode outright and gives the economy a consumable sink that never saturates.
- (decision) CONFIRMED FR-6.2 - Passage always advances to the START of the next environment. If the player is near the end of the current environment it instead carries them further, to the nearest next shortcut door. The jump extends past the boundary; Passage is never wasted on a trivial hop.
- (decision) RESOLVED Q14 - flying over a lever does NOT flick it. The shortcut stays closed. Passage costs the player the loot AND the unlock, so gems can never buy permanent progression and FR-3 'earned by traversal' holds with no asterisk.
- (decision) PASSAGE IS A FAMILY, not one potion. Stated design intents: (a) skip a section the player keeps dying in - an answer to being walled; (b) a Passage variant that travels to the EXIT, for when the player is almost out of air - emergency extraction that banks the bag instead of transforming.
- (event) OPEN Q16 - low-grade gems are now permanently in demand (potion ingredients) but they spawn in the shallow ground that shortcuts exist to skip. Either this is a feature (shortcuts become a real choice rather than an always-take) or a defect (players forced to re-walk ground they earned the right to skip - the exact problem shortcuts were invented to solve). Needs a deliberate answer.
- (event) OPEN Q17 - the exit-Passage potion is a safety valve on the oxygen fail state. Priced cheaply it makes transformation nearly unreachable for anyone with gems, dissolving the fail state the fiction is built on. Its price is load-bearing on the entire timer premise.
- (decision) RUN INTENT established - runs are not homogeneous. A player enters with one of two purposes: (A) RESOURCE RUN, farm known shallow ground for gems to fund future runs, shortcuts deliberately NOT used; or (B) DEPTH RUN, push the frontier, shortcuts used to skip solved ground. Dissolves Q16 - the recipe rule and the shortcut rule do not conflict because they serve different run types. Correction to PM framing, which had assumed every run aims deeper.
- (event) CONSEQUENCE of run intent - the game gains a SECOND success axis. A run that banks low-grade gems and never leaves environment 1 is a successful run. Depth is not the only measure of a good run, which also means the exit is not a compromise on a resource run - it is the plan.
- (event) OPEN Q19 - is run intent EXPLICIT (declared at run start, game acknowledges it with objectives/loadout/UI) or purely EMERGENT (player decides privately, game never knows)? Different scope, different UX, different telemetry.
- (event) OPEN Q20 - a resource run is by construction the least tense content in the game: mastered ground, no frontier, low risk, low information. Risk that run type A is boring by design and becomes the grind players resent. Likely lever is the modifier set (Q9) - resource runs need a reason to be played rather than endured.
- (decision) POTIONS REPOSITIONED - potions are a DIFFICULTY ASSIST, not an economic engine. Small catalogue. Not mandatory: a veteran can beat the game with bad gear and no potions. Normal need arises only when a player is stuck. Potions are the ONLY system requiring mixed gem types. Corrects the PM's earlier framing of potions as the loop's continuous sink.
- (decision) RESOLVED Q20 - resource runs are INFREQUENT, not a standing tax. Three reasons: potions are optional and rarely needed; depth runs also open chests and yield gems along the way; and deep chests can roll shallow loot. What motivates a resource run is anticipation of the NEXT run - the preparation is charged by what it enables, not by its own tension.
- (decision) LOOT VARIANCE IS BIDIRECTIONAL - chest quality is distance-WEIGHTED, not distance-locked. A shallow chest can roll a legendary; a deep chest can roll front-of-dungeon junk. Every chest stays a lottery in both directions, and it means depth runs passively supply low-grade gems.
- (event) Q1 PARTIALLY REOPENED as Q21 - potions being optional and rare means the gem sink is real but SMALL. It absorbs low-grade gems for struggling players, who buy potions, and barely at all for veterans, who do not. The Archero end-state is therefore deferred rather than eliminated for exactly the cohort that plays most.
- (event) OPEN Q22 - 'a veteran can beat the game with bad gear and no potions' is a claim about COMBAT DEPTH, and it is load-bearing. It requires combat skilled enough that mastery substitutes for stats. It also sits in tension with the brief's 'gear raises reachable distance', which is the spine of the progression and monetization model. Both can be true only if gear lowers the skill floor rather than raising the ceiling.
- (decision) COMBAT SPECIFIED (closes Q5). Movement: left/right run, up jump, down crouch. Slide is context-sensitive - forward while moving, large backstep while standing still or attacking. Attacks: sword combo; two-stage bow (press to aim with trajectory preview, press again to fire); jump+down smash. Defence: block opens a 1.5s window, perfect timing parries - melee parry damages the attacker, arrow parry reflects it back at the archer. Any attack is cancellable into slide or block.
- (decision) ENEMIES ARE BUILT FROM THE PLAYER'S OWN VERB SET - difficulty is verb breadth, not stat inflation. Goblin has move + one attack; environment 5 monsters slide and can block the player's attacks. The game therefore never teaches a second vocabulary: learning to play IS learning to read every enemy.
- (decision) Each environment has its own monsters and its own traps, and ENDS WITH A MINI-BOSS slightly stronger than that environment's regular monsters. Five environments, five mini-bosses - gives the run coarse resolved beats and gives the frontier a name players can use.
- (decision) RESOLVED Q22 - 'veteran' is defined as someone who has FINISHED the game and plays PERFECTLY with no mistakes. Gear absorbs mistakes, so a perfect player needs none and everyone else needs gear in proportion to their error rate. Gear raises the FLOOR; skill sets the ceiling. Reconciles with the brief's 'gear raises reachable distance' and gives the business model its strongest anti-pay-to-win line: the ceiling is skill-defined and cannot be bought.
- (event) COMBAT CLOSES THE TIMER THESIS. The Timer section asserted timer -> rushing -> mistakes -> damage -> death but could not name the mistake. It now can: the 1.5s block window is a precise quantity of attention, and the clock is what steals it. Timer and combat are one system, not two.
- (event) OPEN Q25/Q26 raised on the new combat spec - Q25 touch controls, nine distinct inputs against a phone-on-a-bus session profile; Q26 block integrity, a 1.5s window with no cooldown or whiff cost makes mashing block dominant and dissolves the parry skill the design rests on. Also Q23 (does Passage skip mini-bosses) and Q24 (perfect players buy no gear).

- (decision) RESOLVED Q26 - block retuned to conventional action-game numbers. Parry window ~0.3s; a mistimed block locks the player out of movement and dodging for ~0.4s afterwards. Supersedes the earlier 1.5s window. Mashing block is now punished harder than simply taking the hit, which preserves the timing skill the design rests on.
- (decision) STUN ATTACK added as the guard-breaker (answers the FR-7.3 gap). Longer wind-up than a normal attack, substantially less damage, and on connect it stuns the target for ~1 second during which it cannot act. Its value is CONVERSION, not damage. A long wind-up also makes it the most timer-sensitive input in the game - a calm player lands and converts, a rushed player eats the recovery.
- (assumption) Timing values 0.3s parry / 0.4s punish / 1s stun are explicitly PROVISIONAL playtest starting points per Crusher. What must survive tuning is their relationship - parry shorter than punish, stun long enough to convert - not the constants.
- (event) TUNING CONSTRAINT flagged for Q9 - if stun plus a free combo out-damages an unopened combo against any enemy, stun becomes the dominant opener and the sword's speed advantage never matters. The intended cost is the wind-up being punishable when other enemies are on the field, so the balancing lever is ENCOUNTER COMPOSITION rather than the stun's own numbers.
- (event) OPEN Q27 (do mini-bosses and the final boss resist stun) and Q28 (is there a non-parry successful block, or is it parry-or-eat-it). Q25 touch controls escalated - the input count is now ten, several needing 0.3s precision, against a phone form factor.
- (decision) RESOLVED Q25 - INPUT MODEL. The player chooses their device on entering the game. PC plays on keyboard with FULLY CUSTOMIZABLE bindings. Phone plays a split on-screen layout, movement left and attacks right, and the on-screen buttons are CUSTOMIZABLE IN SIZE AND POSITION. Both surfaces are player-configurable, which is the correct posture for a game whose defining skill is a 0.3s read - hands and phones differ, and a fixed layout silently taxes whoever it fits worst.
- (event) DESIGN CONSEQUENCE of FR-12.4 - resizable/movable touch controls are the mitigation for right-thumb density (sword, bow, stun, block under one thumb) and for occlusion (the attack cluster sits where the fight is). Therefore the control-customization screen is a FIRST-CLASS UX SURFACE, not a settings afterthought, and the default layout is a starting point rather than the design. Carry into the UX phase.
- (event) OPEN Q29 raised - CROSS-DEVICE TIMING PARITY. Touch has no tactile feedback and higher latency, so a 0.3s parry window fair on keyboard may be unreachable on glass; the usual fix is a wider touch window. But the brief locks leaderboards (v2) and 1v1 PvP (v3) with normalized starting conditions. Per-device timing values would mean cross-device competition compares two different games. A tuning constant today, a competitive-integrity crisis in v2. Also Q30 - is device choice remembered, auto-detected, or re-asked.
- (decision) RESOLVED Q29 - combat timing values are UNIVERSAL, identical on PC and phone, tuned to be as fair as possible on both. Protects v2 leaderboards and v3 PvP from comparing two different games. Accepted cost: a universal window is effectively set by what is reachable on glass, so the keyboard experience is defined by the phone ceiling, and no timing number may be signed off on desktop alone.
- (decision) RESOLVED Q4 - GOLD is the universal substitute currency. It has no job of its own; it covers SHORTFALLS, standing in for gems the player lacks. The gold cost of covering a gem scales with grade, so high-grade coverage costs substantially more. Sources: chest drops on a chance, plus a small award for winning a 1v1 PvP match (v3). Gems stay specific and do three jobs; gold is deliberately generic.
- (event) MONETIZATION SHAPE - substitution sells TIME, not power: everything gold buys is reachable in gems by playing, which satisfies the brief's locked constraint literally rather than merely in spirit, and matches the Path of Exile convenience precedent from the research. Single legible path: money -> gold -> covers a shortfall -> gear already being worked toward. The gold-per-gem rate by grade is now the single most commercially sensitive number in the game.
- (event) OPEN Q31 (can gold cover 100% of a purchase - the line between money saves a run and money skips the game; the entire no-pay-to-win claim rests on it), Q32 (PvP is v3, so chests are gold's only v1 faucet, making chest drop rates the sole determinant of the free player's earn-vs-buy ratio), Q33 (substitution is one-way - gems cannot become gold; reversing it would close Q21).
- (change) Second FR renumber - input/device moved to FR-9 so IDs run in document order. Chest variance FR-10, potions FR-11, Passage FR-12, gold FR-13. Cross-references updated and verified.
- (decision) RESOLVED Q31 - THE 70% RULE. Gold may only cover a shortfall when the player already holds at least 70% of the required gems; below that threshold gold cannot be spent on the purchase at all. Money can therefore never buy more than the last 30% of anything. Converts the no-purchase-exclusive-power promise from rhetoric into checkable arithmetic, and makes gold a finisher rather than a substitute.
- (event) CONSEQUENCE of the 70% rule - high-grade gems are the rare ones, so reaching 70% of a high-grade requirement is the hard part of any expensive purchase. Gold therefore helps LEAST where players want help MOST. Good for integrity, and a real ceiling on revenue per player that the business model should expect rather than discover.
- (event) ETHICS NOTE recorded in the PRD - the 70% rule means the only state in which gold is spendable is the near-miss state, which the economy research documents as recruiting win-related brain circuitry. Nothing here approaches the Nevada 1989 near-miss line: the threshold is fixed and disclosed and progress toward it is earned, not engineered per player. Logged so the choice is made with open eyes.
- (event) OPEN Q34 - is the 70% threshold measured PER GRADE or ACROSS THE WHOLE COST? Aggregate measurement leaves a loophole: a player holding every common gem and no rare ones can clear 70% and then buy the entire rare portion with gold, which is exactly what FR-13.2a exists to prevent. Per-grade measurement holds everywhere. One line decides whether the guarantee is real.
- (decision) RESOLVED Q34 - the 70% threshold is measured PER GRADE, not across the purchase as a whole. A multi-grade purchase must be at 70% of each grade before gold applies to any of it. Closes the stockpiling loophole: hoarding common gems can never unlock gold spending on the rare portion.
- (decision) PURCHASE COST SHAPES - requirement complexity is reserved for the top end. Ordinary gear and weapons need a SINGLE gem grade. Only very strong gear and weapons need multiple grades in combination. Potions always need multiple grades regardless of how minor they are, making them the exception. Keeps the early game legible, puts the per-grade rule exactly where a pay-to-win loophole would do most damage, and keeps mixed-grade demand alive for players who never reach the top tier.
- (event) OPEN Q35 - do the multiple grades required by top-tier gear include LOW grades as well as high ones? If yes, Q21 largely closes because a veteran's surplus grade-1 gems get consumed by exactly the endgame purchases veterans pursue, and the economy self-balances at the top end. If top gear needs several high grades only, Q21 stays fully open.
- (decision) RESOLVED Q35 and therefore Q21 - top-tier gear requires LOW grades as well as high ones (FR-14.4). Endgame purchases consume a veteran's grade-1 surplus, so the economy self-balances at the top end with no conversion mechanic needed. Also means resource runs survive into the endgame rather than being a beginner's phase, because the best items are partly made of cheap material.

- (decision) RESOLVED Q10 - run validation is CHECKPOINTED + PLAUSIBILITY-BOUNDED with replay logs captured from day one. Server issues the seed and records start time; client posts signed checkpoints; server rejects impossible progress; server COMPUTES THE REWARD ITSELF and never trusts client-claimed loot; balances are never client-written and land in an append-only ledger keyed by run id so duplicates fail as constraint violations. Decision shaped directly by Crusher's zero-fixed-cost constraint, not by capability.

- (event) PLATFORM TRUST CLARIFIED for the record - a native app does not change the trust model. Memory editors, APK repacking and function hooking are mature and free, and mobile cheating scales through distribution of modded clients in a way browser cheating does not. Play Integrity and App Attest verify the app, not the gameplay. Validation is required on every surface. Also noted: the trigger for Q10 is real money in the economy, not the platform.
- (event) ACCEPTED GAP for v1 - checkpoints and plausibility bounds do not stop BOTS. Accepted deliberately: a bot earns at human speed, so it can grind but cannot mint currency, and damage stays bounded by wall-clock. Statistical alerting on gold-per-hour and depth-per-second is the later answer and depends on the NFR-1.4 telemetry.
- (decision) COSMETICS MUST BE SERVER-AUTHORITATIVE TOO (FR-16), raised by Crusher. Threat model differs from currency: a player who hacks a skin onto their own screen has fooled only themselves, but a hacked cosmetic VISIBLE TO OTHERS devalues everyone who paid and destroys the primary revenue line. Visibility is the asset, so visibility is what must be authoritative. Ownership is a server record; entitlements granted only by verified payment webhook or validated store receipt; grants idempotent by transaction id; equipped state validated server-side; and what other players see is served from the server's record, never relayed from the wearing client.
- (event) FR-16.5 is forward-looking - it only bites in v2 (leaderboards) and v3 (PvP), the first surfaces where players see each other. Written now because it is a schema decision today and a migration later. Also partially answers the discovery-phase tension that cosmetics carry 50-80% of revenue only where seen while our visibility surfaces postdate v1: whatever that sequencing costs, it must not also cost cosmetic integrity.
